Anthropic has launched an institutional investor roadshow to raise up to **$64.4 billion** in what would become the largest technology IPO on record, positioning the Claude maker for a **~$1.4 trillion** market valuation under Nasdaq ticker `ANT`.

Updated S-1 registration documents detail plans to sell **825 million Class A shares** marketed at **$70 to $78** apiece. Institutional order books across New York, London, and Singapore were reported roughly six times oversubscribed within 48 hours of marketing launch, with final pricing slated for November 18 and debut trading expected November 19.

## The numbers behind an $81B revenue run rate

What separated Anthropic’s pitch from earlier venture AI cycles is hard enterprise unit growth rather than consumer ad experiments:

- **Revenue inflection:** Annualized revenue jumped from roughly **$9 billion** earlier in the year toward an estimated **$81 billion ARR** pace entering Q4, anchored by production software-engineering contracts and high-throughput API workloads.
- **Enterprise market capture:** Independent developer telemetry tracked on [the Vigas leaderboard](/leaderboard) mirrors Wall Street disclosures — Anthropic holds three of the top four spots overall with `anthropic/claude-fable-5.2` (#1), `anthropic/claude-opus-5.5` (#2), and `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` (#4).
- **Multi-product monetization:** Combining Claude Code subscriptions with direct pay-per-token API tiers stabilized gross margins even as token context windows expanded to 1 million tokens.

## What public-market pressure means for API pricing

Once quarterly earnings calls begin under ticker `ANT`, gross-margin scrutiny intensifies immediately. Wall Street will weigh frontier training clusters against inference margins — likely keeping flagship prices (`Opus 5.5` at $15 / $75 per million tokens) firm while defending high-volume developer routing via mid-tier workhorses like **Claude Fable 5.2** ($1.80 / $9.00) and **Sonnet 5.5** ($3 / $15).

For startups and engineering leaders budgeting Q1 2027 compute on [Vigas model pricing](/models), vendor concentration risk is the practical takeaway. Keeping an OpenAI-compatible routing layer lets teams route critical code generation to Anthropic while off-loading bulk classification or summarization to lower-cost providers like Google Gemini or DeepSeek whenever margins shift.
